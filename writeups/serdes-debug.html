GTM-to-GTYP SerDes Protocol Debug

Diagnosed and resolved a critical high-speed serial bug in proprietary chip-to-chip communication protocol before release

Problem

During a routine test of our proprietary chip-to-chip protocol: running custom protocol words in both directions using GTM and GTYP transceivers, the receive side PMD was failing to align in both directions.

Investigation

Objective was to narrow down possible causes with datapath tests. Initial waveform inspection showed the sync bit and header bits appearing in the wrong order, and spliced across the width of the bus.

Possible causes I identified:

  • Differing parallel to serial bit-ordering between GTM family and GTYP transceivers
  • Some issue introduced by the protocol word structure itself, incompatibility with the internal serialization process
  • Gearboxing on the GTM side, from 80 to the protocol bit width and back, could introduce bit ordering issues or present spliced parts of words

I used WMI simulation drivers, as well as PRBS stimulus generators and checkers, to validate portions of the datapath as shown below. Hover over failure points to see how I eliminated them as potential causes.

Root cause

An earlier artifact had been left in from the development of the protocol's configuration for GTM transceivers: a bit-reversal prior to gearboxing, and bit-reversal on receive post-gearboxing, included to achieve correct bit-ordering for the gearbox.

This led to an anomaly where GTM loopback simulations were succeeding and passing verification, but any other configuration attempting to work with a protocol block configured with GTM would either receive bit-reversed protocol data or send legitimate protocol data that would then be bit-reversed.

Fix and results

First, I confirmed the issue by forcing an additional bit-reversal into the cross-connect configuration. The receive PMDs on either side locked up, and PRBS data was checked correctly. This confirmed that the issue was the bit-reversal.

Working with an existing GTM loopback simulation, I bypassed the bit-reversal and ran PRBS across in simulation, proving that there was no need for the bit-reversals before and after the gearboxing step. My mentor also confirmed this in hardware. I pushed up the changes to the GTM configuration and a fix to the GTYP PMD module, and finally verified cross-family compatibility prior to release.

Takeaways

My main professional takeaway was that I need to trust my technical instincts more quickly. Despite me noticing what appeared to be a bit-reversal early on, in discussions with my mentor we were unconvinced that a bit-reversal was possible, as no mechanism in the protocol supposedly modified bit ordering.

Had I forced a bit-reversal earlier on, I would have identified the issue and been able to close this project much sooner than I did.

I also learned the importance of working incrementally from functional designs. I burnt a lot of time on this project trying to make an initial simulation work, when several parts of it were constantly falling apart because they hadn't been tested.

My final simulation was built from the ground up, starting with just the two SerDes modules and expanded to include protocol blocks and PRBS generators/checkers, verified as I went. This meant I could isolate bugs and catch issues before they propagated through the rest of the system.
